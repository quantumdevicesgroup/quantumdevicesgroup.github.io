Superconducting circuits are the leading solid-state platform for quantum computing, and their coherence is limited by the qubit's microscopic environment: two-level-system defects in materials and interfaces, and the phonons that these defects and the qubit itself can emit. We treat the qubit, its defect bath and its phonon bath as one coupled electromechanical system, build microscopic models of how each limits coherence, and use them to engineer compact qubits with longer lifetimes.
